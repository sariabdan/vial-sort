# Training

## Scope

This record owns how the models were trained: the training runs and their loss curves as recorded, the hardware and duration of each, the retrains on the university cluster, the normalisation and the pinning of its quantiles, the image augmentations, the short fine-tunes and patched checkpoints, the offline probes (what each tests and what it found), and why the first models read the destination off the camera picture.

It does not own:

- what each model is, its lineage, its full settings and where it is published: the [models record](../models/models.md);
- the demonstrations, the trims and the wordings as data: the [demonstrations record](../demonstrations/demonstrations.md);
- the rig and cameras: the [task and rig record](../task-and-rig/task-and-rig.md);
- how rollouts on the arm are run and scored, and the robot results: the [evaluation record](../evaluation/evaluation.md) and the [results record](../results/results.md);
- the dated write-ups of the retrains and arm tests: the [Lab notes](../../experiments/experiments.md);
- how the Training page draws all this: the [website record](../website/website.md).

## Where the numbers come from

The loss curves are in `src/data/training.json`, one point every 100 steps per run with the loss and the gradient norm (FLUX runs also carry `action_mse`). The offline gate of Mango Rice is in `src/data/gate.json`; the probe of 21 October in `src/lib/probe.ts`; run facts in `src/data/registry.json`; settings in `src/data/experiments.json`; the stories in `models.yaml`. The cluster retrains and their probes are written up in the four Lab notes under `docs/experiments/`.

The JSON under `src/data/` is generated by `scripts/build_data.py` from training logs, `train_config.json` files, operator notes and robot recordings that are not in this repository (they are in the `~/lerobot` checkout on the workstation). The numbers below are as recorded in that data, in `models.yaml`, `src/lib/probe.ts` and the Lab notes at commit 8065309 (2026-11-28). They were not re-derived, and none was checked against the logs.

Model names are the website's; the Lab notes' "A" is Plain Bagel and "B" is Mango Pudding (the full list of older names is in the [models record](../models/models.md#all-models)).

## Loss curves

The Training page (`src/pages/training.astro`, drawn by `src/charts/training.ts`) puts one chart per kind of model, because each kind measures its loss differently; runs of one kind share a log-scale axis.

| Kind | What the curve is (`src/charts/training.ts`) |
|---|---|
| π0.5 | loss |
| FLUX 3 Action | loss (video + action); `action_mse` is shown as "action error". FLUX learns to predict future video as well as actions, with loss weights video 1, action 0.5, gripper channel ×2 (`src/data/experiments.json`) |
| Isaac 0.5 | flow loss of the action expert |

What the curves can and cannot say:

- There is no validation loss for the π0.5 and FLUX runs: every demonstration is used for training, so a curve cannot show when a model starts to memorise (`src/data/experiments.json`). The Training page gives the reason as "every demonstration has its own wording". The robot is the only test.
- Isaac 0.5 Expert is the exception: 8 demonstrations were held out and scored offline (see [the offline probes](#the-offline-probes)).
- Loss says how well the model fits the recorded actions, not how it behaves on the arm (`docs/experiments/2026-11-07-retrains-on-the-cluster/retrains-on-the-cluster.md`; glossary entry "Loss").
- Crispy Falafel's loss was flat from about 8,000 steps (`models.yaml`).
- Plain Bagel and Mango Pudding both ended near 0.009 although they started from different weights; their robot results differ widely (the [results record](../results/results.md)).

## Normalisation and the quantile pinning

### How each family scales its data

| Family | Normalisation (`src/data/experiments.json`) |
|---|---|
| π0.5 | State and action rescaled with the 1st and 99th percentile (q01/q99) of the training data set; images unchanged |
| Isaac 0.5 | The checkpoint's own 1st and 99th percentiles kept, widened only where this data reaches further, so joint values mean what they meant to it before |
| FLUX 3 Action | Built into the base; the base's three normalisation files were rewritten with this data set's 1st and 99th percentiles (`prepare_base.py`). Flux five used the shipped joint ranges, which did not fit the arm; Sweet Corn was the first FLUX run with the arm's own statistics |

### The mismatch that broke Mango Lassi

From `docs/experiments/2026-11-04-b4000-arm-test/b4000-arm-test.md`:

- Mango Lassi continued Mango Rice on the merged 220 demonstrations. A fine-tune inherits the scale of the model it starts from.
- Merging the datasets changed the scale of the two joints that do the grasp:

| Joint | Span in Mango Rice's data | Span in the 220 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| gripper | 37.4° | 50.8° | +36% |
| wrist_flex | 146.4° | 134.9° | −8% |
| the other four | unchanged | unchanged | none |

- The cause of the wider span: LeRobot's merge does not recompute percentiles; it takes a min/max envelope over the per-episode statistics. 15 of the 220 episodes (9 from the 28 Oct contrast set, 6 from the depth set) open the gripper past 37.4°, up to 50.9°, and set the scale for all 220. The true 99th percentile over the merged frames is 36.5°. Both datasets have envelope-style quantiles; what breaks a fine-tune is that the number changed.
- Effect on the gripper: a command Mango Rice meant as 5.0° became 6.8°, 8.0° became 10.9° ("a grip becomes a loose hold"), 12.0° became 16.3°. On the arm: "failed pickup (but not missed)".
- Effect on the wrist: about −4.3° at the typical grasp pitch of −49° (−0.3° at −100°, −8.2° at 0°, −9.8° at +20°). In the unpatched checkpoint the two errors partly cancelled.
- Not the cause, per the note: other users' GPU and CPU load (26.5 ms of spare time per control tick), the trimming, the depth episodes (depth is not in the training set), or the episode-141 label correction.

### The fix and the test

| Version | gripper q99 | wrist_flex q99 | Grasp, on its own rollouts (4 Nov Lab note) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mango Lassi (as trained) | 50.8 (wrong) | 31.5 (wrong) | 60% (21 of 35, varied tasks) |
| Mango Chutney (`004000_q37g`) | 37.4 (right) | 31.5 (wrong) | 0% (0 of 4, Mango Sorbet's hard cases) |
| Mango Sorbet (`004000_q37`) | 37.4 (right) | 43.2 (right) | 90% (9 of 10, varied tasks) |

The patch was made with `tools/pin_quantiles.py` on the checkpoint, weights untouched and hardlinked. The decision recorded: pin all six joints in the dataset before retraining (the tool's default; "do NOT pass `--joints 5`"); the earlier suggestion to pin only the gripper was withdrawn as the worst of the three options. Both cluster retrains then used the original percentiles, pinned in the dataset before training, for all six joints (7 Nov Lab note). Plain Bagel, trained from the base, has no inherited scale, so for it the mismatch cannot happen (4 Nov Lab note). `tools/pin_quantiles.py` is not in this repository.

After the retrain, the 8 Nov Lab note checked the recorded gripper commands of Mango Pudding: every grasp that picked the vial up was commanded shut (−3.0 to +0.6°), the jaws stopped at 5.8 to 10.2° on the vial and held for 3.6 to 43 s, so "the 4 Nov scale problem is gone".

## The retrains on the university cluster

From `docs/experiments/2026-11-07-retrains-on-the-cluster/retrains-on-the-cluster.md`.

| Run | Starts from | Learning rate | Started | Finished | Loss at step 100 → end |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B (Mango Pudding) | the 12k checkpoint of the earlier 100-demonstration run (Mango Rice) | 1e-5 | Fri 6 Nov, 13:41 | Sat 7 Nov, 11:18 | 0.085 → about 0.009 |
| A (Plain Bagel) | the public `pi05_base` | 2.5e-5 | Sat 7 Nov, 01:34 | Sat 7 Nov, 23:10 | 0.099 → about 0.009 |

Common to both: every weight of π0.5 trained on the same 220 demonstrations (113,010 frames after the idle start and end are trimmed), operator-free starts, batch 16, 16,000 steps (2.3 passes), a checkpoint every 1,000 steps, the same augmentations as before, quantiles pinned for all six joints.

How it ran:

- **GPUs:** the account may use only the A100 nodes, two 80 GB cards; both were used at once, one per run. A π0.5 full fine-tune peaks at about 38 GiB at batch 16.
- **12-hour jobs:** each job saves a checkpoint, works out whether the next one would still fit in its time limit, and if not stops and hands over to a job it queued beforehand, which resumes from the last checkpoint. Each run took two jobs. A chained test with 40 steps was run first.
- **Speed:** 4.8 s per step on one A100, limited by the GPU rather than by data loading, so the two runs did not slow each other down.
- **Memory:** the default AdamW update briefly needs an extra copy of the weights (about 37 GiB at any batch size); the fused AdamW implementation does the same arithmetic without it, which is why it was switched on.
- **Video decoding:** the cluster has no FFmpeg libraries for torchcodec, so frames are decoded with PyAV; "the frames are the same".
- **Offline checks:** after training, three probes ran on checkpoints 8,000 and 10,000 to 16,000 of both runs, on the cluster, in about 80 minutes each. They were flat across checkpoints, so none beat the final one. Results under [the offline probes](#the-offline-probes).

The note cites `ee/CLUSTER_CLAUSTHAL.md` for the cluster; that file is not in this repository.

### The offline gate of Mango Rice

From `src/data/gate.json`. The check definitions are the hover texts of the Training page (`src/pages/training.astro`):

- **Leaves home:** shown the first frame of nine real rollouts, with the arm at home, does it predict a move away? Counted when all three random seeds do. Frames from the start of a demonstration move 55 to 90°; a model that will sit still moves a few degrees.
- **Reach follows the source slot:** vials stand in right-rack slots 1, 3 and 6 and the command names each in turn. The predicted shoulder angle 1.7 s ahead must order the slots as the demonstrations do (1 furthest, then 3, then 6) with at least 15° between the ends.
- **Binds the two numbers to their roles:** "from position 3 … to slot 1" and "from position 1 … to slot 3" use the same two numbers; a model that understands which is the source reaches differently for them, by at least 8°.
- **Reads the destination slot:** 7 frames taken one second after a grasp, the same vial each time, with commands that ask for destination slots 1, 3 and 6. A frame passes when the predicted reach is ordered as in the demonstrations, by at least 10°; the check passes when at least 4 of the 7 do.
- **Insertion and gripper error:** degrees between the predicted motion while placing and the recorded one, on 15 demonstrations.

The recorded shoulder (pan) angles for source slots 1, 3 and 6 in the demonstrations are 36, 21 and 3°.

### The destination probe of 21 October

From `src/lib/probe.ts`: how often Mango Rice does what the row says, on training frames.

| Test | At 12k | At 2k |
|---|---|---|
| Replays its own demonstration's destination | 100% | 88% |
| The command names another source vial: it goes there | 94% | 94% |
| The command names another destination: it follows it | 19% | 31% |

## Why the first models read the destination off the picture

From the Training page's "Why the destination fails" tab, the home page's chapter 02 (`src/pages/index.astro`) and the Mango Rice card in `scripts/upload_hf.py`:

1. The first 100 demonstrations (`vial-sort-slots-100`) were recorded one destination per sitting, with the bin and the light changing between sittings (the card).
2. The camera picture says which sitting a frame is from.
3. The sitting says the destination, because each sitting had only one.
4. So the destination is read off the picture, and the words that name it are not needed.

The source slot was learned because it changed within a sitting, so the picture could not give it away. The evidence is the 21 Oct probe (a prompt naming another destination followed 19% of the time, swapping all five camera images steering it 81%) and the offline gate (0 of 7 destination frames at every checkpoint while the source check passed).

The FLUX model shows the same split: source slots are read, destination slots are not; `models.yaml` adds that a rack of six slots covers about two of the 8 by 8 cells FLUX divides its picture into.

The fix was in the data, not the training: in the newer demonstrations one layout is recorded three times, to three different destinations, so only the command can tell them apart (home page chapter 02; 150 of the 220 per the Plain Bagel card). On the arm Mango Pudding's destination then followed the command, and Plain Bagel, trained from the base on the same data, reached slots no demonstration used as a destination (the [results record](../results/results.md)). The 8 Nov Lab note gives the reason for training Plain Bagel from the base: it "never had base12k's habit of reading the scene".
