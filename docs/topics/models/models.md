# Models

## Scope

This record owns every model of the project: its name, the names it had in the Lab notes, its family, what it started from, which demonstrations it was trained on, which cameras it saw, its checkpoints, its settings as recorded, where it is published, and its story (why it was trained and what it showed). It also lists the earlier policies of the August work and the models planned or considered.

It does not own:

- how the runs were trained, their loss curves, the cluster retrains, the offline probes and the normalisation fix in detail: the [training record](../training/training.md);
- the demonstrations and their commands: the [demonstrations record](../demonstrations/demonstrations.md);
- the arm, the racks and the cameras: the [task and rig record](../task-and-rig/task-and-rig.md);
- how a rollout is run and scored: the [evaluation record](../evaluation/evaluation.md);
- the full robot results, run by run: the [results record](../results/results.md). The robot outcomes quoted here are the short accounts that the model's own sources give;
- the dated arm tests and retrains as written at the time: the [Lab notes](../../experiments/experiments.md);
- how the website shows the models: the [website record](../website/website.md).

## Where the facts come from

`models.yaml` is the only place a model gets a name. `scripts/build_data.py` joins each entry to its robot sessions (by its run id), its training log and its `train_config.json`, and writes `src/data/registry.json`, `src/data/experiments.json` (the same models with their settings and notes), `src/data/training.json` (the loss curves) and `src/data/models.json` (the comparison table, including the August, planned and considered models). The Models page (`src/pages/models.astro`, `src/components/ModelDetail.astro`) renders `src/data/experiments.json`.

The JSON under `src/data/` is generated by scripts from training logs, configs, operator notes and robot recordings that are not in this repository (they live in the `~/lerobot` checkout on the workstation). This record does not copy the generated values or `models.yaml` row by row: a copy goes stale with every rebuild of the data. Where a section's values sit in one file, the section says which file and key hold them and which page shows them; the numbers the record argues from are kept as [readings at commit 8065309](#readings-at-commit-8065309). Every number that remains below is as recorded in that data, and in `models.yaml` and the Lab notes, at commit 8065309 (2026-11-28). None was re-derived. The header of `models.yaml` names its own sources: `ee/MODELS_SEP_OCT_2026.md` (23 Oct 2026) and the training logs, neither of which is in this repository.

Terms follow the website's glossary (`src/lib/glossary.ts`): rollout, session, checkpoint, step, pass over the data, demonstration, command, move, slot, full fine-tune, LoRA, EMA weights, sync mode, RTC, offline gate, operator-free start.

## Per-model facts at a glance

Each model is one entry of `models` in `src/data/registry.json`, keyed by `id` and joined to its robot sessions by `run`. `src/data/experiments.json` holds the same entries with their settings and notes added. This record does not copy the values: they change whenever the data is rebuilt.

| Fact | Field of the model's entry | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Frames | `frames` | The size of the training copy (from `models.yaml`) |
| Passes | `passes` | Steps × batch size ÷ frames, as the data records it; `null` when not recorded |
| Training time, time per step, peak GPU memory | `hours`, `step_s`, `mem_gb` | From the training log; `null` for the models with no training of their own or no log |
| Steps | `steps` | Optimiser steps of the run |
| Rollouts | `rollouts` | The scored robot rollouts the site joins to the model |
| Tested checkpoints | `checkpoints` | The checkpoints with scored robot rollouts |
| The model's runs on the arm | `rows` | Run keys of `rows` in `src/data/results.json` |
| Settings read from | `hyper_source` | The `train_config.json` saved with the checkpoint, or the project notes where the checkpoint and its config were deleted |
| Rollouts left out of the site's counts, and why | `left_out` | Counts by reason: `restart`, `short`, `unreadable`, `earlier_scene` |
| Whether its rollouts can be scored | `scored` | `false` for Sweet Corn, whose rollouts are on the earlier scene layout |

The Models page (`src/pages/models.astro`, `src/components/ModelDetail.astro`) shows these per model, and the comparison table (`src/components/ModelTable.astro`) shows the `src/data/models.json` rows. The training time, time per step and memory of each run are also in `src/data/training.json` and drawn on the Training page; see the [training record](../training/training.md#the-runs).

## Settings shared by the π0.5 runs

The settings of each model are `hyper` of its entry in `src/data/experiments.json`. `scripts/build_data.py` writes them with `hyper_pi05`, `hyper_flux` or `hyper_isaac` from the `train_config.json` saved with the checkpoint; for Pretzel One and Pretzel Two, whose checkpoints and configs were deleted, they are filled in from the project notes (`hyper_source` says which). `hyper` is a list of groups, each a `group` name and `rows` of three strings: the setting, its value and what it means. The Models page shows them under each model in `src/components/ModelDetail.astro`.

| Group (`group`) | Settings in it, for the π0.5 runs |
|---|---|
| What trains | Starting weights, parameters trained, precision |
| Optimiser | Optimiser, peak learning rate, betas, weight decay, gradient clipping |
| Learning-rate schedule | Shape, warm-up steps, decay steps, final learning rate |
| Batches and length | Batch size, steps, passes over the data, validation split |
| What it sees and predicts | Cameras, image size, prompt length, action chunk, denoising steps, normalisation |
| Image augmentation | Augmentation |
| Reproducibility | Seed |

The seven π0.5 models with a saved `train_config.json` (Plain Bagel, Mango Pudding, Mango Lassi, Mango Sorbet, Mango Chutney, Spicy Chicken, Mango Rice) share every setting except a few; the differences are in each model's section and under [readings](#readings-at-commit-8065309). The π0.5 config sets warm-up to 1,000 steps and decay to 30,000, and both are scaled to fit the run. What the shared settings are, in plain words, is in the [training record](../training/training.md) (image augmentation, normalisation) and on the Models page.

The Hugging Face cards written by `scripts/upload_hf.py` add: inputs are five 640×480 colour cameras, the 6 joint positions and the prompt; output is 6 joint targets in chunks of 50, run with `n_action_steps=15`.

## Isaac 0.5 Expert

- **Notes name:** none in this repository. Run id `isaac-i21k`.
- **Family:** Isaac 0.5, by Perceptron.
- **Started from:** `PerceptronAI/Isaac-0.5-SO100_101`, Perceptron's Isaac 0.5, already fine-tuned by them on SO-100 and SO-101 arms.
- **Trained on:** `vial-sort-slots-220` minus 8 held out (108,253 frames), idle start and end cut, operator removed from the wrist camera's start frames, one sentence per move.
- **Cameras:** top_down and wrist. The checkpoint has exactly two camera slots, so it sees two of the five cameras.
- **Checkpoints:** saved every 5,250 steps (5,250 to 21,000); the last, 21,000, was kept and run on the robot.
- **Training log and config:** `isaac/isaac_expert_ws212_b12_21000.log`; `isaac_expert_ws212_b12_21000/checkpoints/021000/pretrained_model/train_config.json` (paths under the workstation's `outputs/`).
- **Published:** no (`hub` is empty).

Settings: `hyper` of its entry (`isaac-expert`) in `src/data/experiments.json`, read from the `train_config.json` saved with the checkpoint; the Models page shows them. The story below says why only the action expert trains.

Story (`models.yaml`):

- **Why only the action expert:** training all 36 billion parameters needs far more memory than the project's GPUs have.
- **How it was trained:** 24 to 25 November, on the workstation's RTX PRO 6000 (96 GB), in 13 hours; 2.2 s per step, 88 GB of GPU memory. The action expert's flow loss fell from 0.041 to 0.003.
- **Tested without the robot:** on the 8 held-out demonstrations its predicted next second is 2.8° off per joint on average, against 5.2° for holding still and 8.4° for the checkpoint before this training; the gripper opened or closed the right way 19 times in 21. The four saved checkpoints score within 0.15° of each other.
- **On the robot, 25 to 27 November:** 42 moves scored from the operator's notes, in eight blocks of five or six moves from one source slot (L1, R1, L3, R3, L4, R4, L6, R6) to the other rack, 30 of them to slots 2, 4 and 5. In the asked slot in 20 of 42; to slots 1, 3 and 6: 11 of 12; to slot 2: 9 of 10; to slots 4 and 5: 0 of 20 (slot 3 fifteen times, slot 4 five times). The right vial was picked up in 41 of 42. The bin and source slots 2 and 5 were not tried. It plans 60 actions in about half a second and runs 30, about 21 actions a second on the arm. A first test session of 4 moves on 25 November has no notes and is not counted.

## Plain Bagel

- **Notes name:** "A", "A at 16000" (`docs/experiments/2026-11-07-retrains-on-the-cluster/retrains-on-the-cluster.md`, `docs/experiments/2026-11-09-a16000-arm-test/a16000-arm-test.md`). Run id `pi05-a16k`.
- **Family:** π0.5.
- **Started from:** the public `lerobot/pi05_base`.
- **Trained on:** `vial-sort-slots-220` (113,010 frames), idle start and end cut, operator removed from the start frames, one sentence per move. The `models.yaml` line: the 220 demonstrations, "where the destination cannot be read off the cameras".
- **Cameras:** all five.
- **Checkpoints:** saved every 1,000 steps; 16,000 kept and run on the robot. The Lab note gives the checkpoint path `outputs/pi05_full_vial-sort-ws220_canon_base16k/checkpoints/016000`.
- **Training logs and config:** `cluster_logs/slurm-pi05-a-37335.out` (from step 0), `cluster_logs/slurm-pi05-a-37336.out` (from 8,000); `pi05_full_vial-sort-ws220_canon_base16k/checkpoints/016000/pretrained_model/train_config.json`.
- **Published:** `sari-abdan/vial-sort-pi05-ws220-a-16000`, with the dataset `sari-abdan/vial-sort-slots-220`.

Settings that differ from those the π0.5 runs share (`hyper` in `src/data/experiments.json`): peak learning rate 2.5e-05; warm-up 533 steps, decay 16,000 steps (both scaled from 1,000 and 30,000); 16,000 steps (2.27 passes); checkpoints every 1,000. Quantiles pinned for all six joints (Lab note of 7 Nov).

Story:

- **Why it was trained:** the 4 Nov Lab note proposed training "A" from `lerobot/pi05_base` on the same 220 demonstrations: with no inherited scale it learns the gripper range of this data from scratch, so the normalisation mismatch that broke Mango Lassi cannot happen, and it gets more passes. The note says this is also the run the operator asked for on 3 Nov ("on the real base").
- **How it was trained:** 7 November, on one A100 80 GB of a university GPU cluster, in two 12-hour jobs; the first stopped itself after the 8,000-step checkpoint and the second resumed from it. Batch 16, learning rate 2.5e-5, 4.8 s per step, 37.8 GiB. Loss fell from 0.099 to 0.009 (`models.yaml`). The Lab note gives the start and finish as Sat 7 Nov 01:34 and 23:10.
- **Tested without the robot:** every checkpoint from 8,000 steps on went through the offline probes; they scored the same within a few points, so the last was kept. Its predicted destination for slots 1, 3 and 6 is further apart than Mango Pudding's on the same frames. The probe details are in the [training record](../training/training.md).
- **On the robot:** 82 moves over 9 and 24 November, scored from the operator's notes: in the asked slot in 51 of 82; the right vial picked up in 77. On 9 November, on the 31 moves Mango Pudding also ran, 21 in the asked slot against 11 (`models.yaml`; the 9 Nov Lab note).

- **Checkpoints:** saved every 1,000 steps; 16,000 run on the robot. Checkpoint path `outputs/pi05_full_vial-sort-ws220_canon_ft012000_v2/checkpoints/016000` (8 Nov Lab note).
- **Training logs and config:** `cluster_logs/slurm-pi05-b-37157.out` (from 0), `cluster_logs/slurm-pi05-b-37256.out` (from 8,000); `pi05_full_vial-sort-ws220_canon_ft012000_v2/checkpoints/016000/pretrained_model/train_config.json`.
- **Published:** `sari-abdan/vial-sort-pi05-ws220-b-16000`. Its card says to use the Plain Bagel checkpoint instead.

Settings that differ from those the π0.5 runs share (`hyper` in `src/data/experiments.json`): peak learning rate 1e-05; warm-up 533, decay 16,000; 16,000 steps (2.27 passes); checkpoints every 1,000. The joint ranges used to scale the data were set back to Mango Rice's own before training.

Story:

- **Why it was trained:** the revised plan of the 4 Nov Lab note: retrain "B" from Mango Rice's 12,000-step checkpoint on the 220 demonstrations with the pinned quantiles, to 16,000 steps rather than 12,000, because the destination was "visibly unbound" after 0.57 of a pass.
- **How it was trained:** 6 to 7 November on one A100 80 GB of the university cluster, in two 12-hour jobs (Fri 6 Nov 13:41 to Sat 7 Nov 11:18). Loss fell from 0.085 to 0.009.
- **On the robot, 8 and 9 November:** 31 moves, 11 in the asked slot. The destination now follows the command: with the vial at R3, slots 1, 3 and 6 sent it to slot 1, about 3 and 5. The far slots were pulled toward the middle, and seven moves failed at the grasp, most of them from slot 6. The 8 Nov Lab note's analysis of the recorded gripper commands says the grasp failures are the model's, not the gripper scale.
- **Offline:** on the probes its checkpoints 8,000 to 16,000 were flat within 1 to 3°, so no earlier checkpoint was better.

## Mango Lassi

- **Notes name:** "B at 4000"; checkpoint `004000` (4 Nov Lab note). Run id `pi05-b4k`.
- **Family:** π0.5.
- **Started from:** Mango Rice at 12,000 steps ("base12k 012000" in the Lab note).
- **Trained on:** `vial-sort-slots-220` (113,010 frames), trimmed, no depth.
- **Cameras:** all five.
- **Checkpoints:** saved every 500 steps; 4,000 run on the robot. Checkpoint path `outputs/pi05_full_vial-sort-ws220_canon_ft012000/checkpoints/004000`.
- **Training log and config:** `pi05_full_vial-sort-ws220_canon_ft012000.log`; `pi05_full_vial-sort-ws220_canon_ft012000/checkpoints/004000/pretrained_model/train_config.json`.
- **Published:** no.

Settings that differ from those the π0.5 runs share (`hyper` in `src/data/experiments.json`): peak learning rate 1e-05; warm-up 133, decay 4,000 (scaled from 1,000 and 30,000); 4,000 steps (0.57 of a pass); checkpoints every 500. Its quantiles were not pinned: the gripper q99 was 50.8° and wrist_flex q99 31.5°.

Story:

- **Why it lost the grasp:** on 4 November it failed at the grasp in 14 of 35 rollouts (Lab note; `models.yaml`). Merging the datasets had widened the gripper's range in the scaling from 37.4° to 50.8°, so every closing command came out about a third too open (the Lab note: about 36% too wide). A model trained on from Mango Rice keeps Mango Rice's scale. The Lab note adds that 4,000 steps at 1e-5 was not enough to recalibrate, and that earlier checkpoints are less adapted, not more, so none was tested.
- **Destination:** the wrong-slot placements fell between the trained destination slots (slot 2 five times, slot 5 twice): it knew "a slot on that rack" but had not bound the digit.
- **What followed:** Mango Sorbet and Mango Chutney are these weights with ranges written back.

## Mango Chutney

- **Notes name:** "004000_q37g". Run id `pi05-b4k-q37g`.
- **Family:** π0.5.
- **Started from:** Mango Lassi, the same weights, with only the gripper range set back (gripper q99 37.4°, wrist_flex left at 31.5).
- **Trained on, cameras, settings:** as Mango Lassi.
- **Checkpoint:** `pi05_full_vial-sort-ws220_canon_ft012000/checkpoints/004000_q37g`.
- **Published:** no.

Story: built to test whether the lift failures of Mango Sorbet came from the wrist_flex half of the patch. On 4 November it failed at the grasp in all four moves, three of them tasks Mango Sorbet had picked up. The wrist pitch range had also changed by 8%, which tilts the gripper about 4° down at the grasp (the Lab note: about 4.3° at a typical grasp pitch of −49°); in the unpatched checkpoint the two errors partly cancelled. Conclusion recorded: both joints have to be set back, and all six are pinned before retraining. The Lab note states the caveat itself: n = 4, and the one task that succeeded on Mango Sorbet was never run on Mango Chutney.

## Spicy Chicken

- **Notes name:** none in this repository. Run id `pi05-allcams`.
- **Family:** π0.5.
- **Started from:** `lerobot/pi05_base`.
- **Trained on:** `vial-sort-slots-100` as recorded: all 58,650 frames, including the idle start of each demonstration, 100 different wordings.
- **Cameras:** all five.
- **Checkpoints:** saved every 1,000 steps; 7,000, 9,000 and 12,000 run on the robot.
- **Training logs and config:** `pi05_train_allcams.log` (from 0), `pi05_resume.log` (from 7,000), `pi05_resume2.log` (from 8,000); `pi05_full_vial-sort-ws100_allcams/checkpoints/012000/pretrained_model/train_config.json`.
- **Published:** `sari-abdan/vial-sort-pi05-ws100-allcams-12000`. Its card says to use the trimmed run's checkpoint instead, unless reproducing the comparison.

Settings that differ from those the π0.5 runs share (`hyper` in `src/data/experiments.json`): peak learning rate 2.5e-05; warm-up 400, decay 12,000; 12,000 steps (3.27 passes); checkpoints every 1,000. The Hugging Face card gives the hardware as one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, about 11.8 hours.

Story:

- **What it was:** the first full fine-tune (Hugging Face card).
- **How it was trained:** 15 to 16 October, in three stretches: stopped at 7,000 steps to stay inside a time budget and at 8,000 for a reboot, resumed from the saved checkpoint each time. Loss fell from 0.125 to 0.009.
- **On the robot:** at 7,000 steps both valid rollouts on 16 October put the vial in the right place. At 12,000 steps it left home in only 2 of 11 rollouts on 19 October; at 9,000 in 0 of 3.
- **Why it froze:** found on 19 October with offline tests: about half of all frames at the home pose are the idle start of a demonstration and half the idle end, from the identical pose, so the policy told them apart from the pictures alone. The operator leaned over the robot at the start of every demonstration, and the policy learned that as its cue to move. Swapping just the wrist or the left_bottom image of a frozen rollout frame for a demonstration start made it reach.
- **What followed:** Pretzel One and Pretzel Two (fine-tunes of its 12,000-step checkpoint) and Mango Rice (a new run from the base on a cleaned copy).

## Mango Rice

- **Notes name:** the Lab notes call the starting point of "B" "base12k" and "the 12k checkpoint of the earlier 100-demonstration run"; `models.yaml` gives that starting point as "Mango Rice at 12k steps". That "base12k" is Mango Rice is read from this match, not stated in one sentence anywhere. Run id `pi05-canon`.
- **Family:** π0.5.
- **Started from:** `lerobot/pi05_base`.
- **Trained on:** a cleaned copy of `vial-sort-slots-100` (46,361 frames, a derived copy that is not published): idle start and end cut, operator removed from the start frames, one canonical sentence per move.
- **Cameras:** all five.
- **Checkpoints:** saved every 1,000 steps, each through the offline gate; 10,000 and 12,000 run on the robot.
- **Training log and config:** `pi05_train_base12k.log`; `pi05_full_vial-sort-ws100_canon_base12k/checkpoints/012000/pretrained_model/train_config.json`.
- **Published:** `sari-abdan/vial-sort-pi05-ws100-canon-12000`.

Settings that differ from those the π0.5 runs share (`hyper` in `src/data/experiments.json`): peak learning rate 2.5e-05; warm-up 400, decay 12,000; 12,000 steps (4.14 passes); checkpoints every 1,000. Hardware per the Hugging Face card: one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, about 11.6 hours.

Story:

- **What is different from Spicy Chicken:** the same recipe from the same base weights, with three changes to the data: the idle start and end of each demonstration are cut (58,650 frames became 46,361); about 8% of the samples at the home pose have the images that showed the operator swapped for views of the arm with nobody near it (the card: the wrist and left_bottom views of the first frames, taken from other demonstrations and from rollout recordings); and the 100 wordings are replaced by 32 canonical sentences, one per move. It trained 4.1 passes against 3.3.
- **Tested every 1,000 steps without the robot:** it leaves home at all 12 checkpoints; its reach follows the source slot named in the command; it never follows the destination slot. The probe of 21 October found why: the 100 demonstrations were recorded one destination per sitting, so the destination is read off the picture. Details in the [training record](../training/training.md).
- **On the robot, 21 October:** run in RTC first, which never lifted the vial with this checkpoint, then in sync mode. In sync mode it took the right vial every time the command was worded exactly as in training, and put it in the asked slot in two of six rollouts, both slot 3.
- **Later:** the starting point of Mango Lassi and Mango Pudding. The 4 Nov Lab note says it "sent every destination to L3".

## Crispy Falafel

- **Notes name:** none in this repository. Run id `flux-ws100`.
- **Family:** FLUX 3 Action, by Black Forest Labs; LoRA fine-tune.
- **Started from:** `black-forest-labs/flux-3-action-so101`, with its three normalisation files rewritten using this dataset's 1st and 99th percentiles (`prepare_base.py`, uploaded with the adapter).
- **Trained on:** `vial-sort-slots-100` (58,650 frames), as a copy with only the top_down camera (as `scene`) and the wrist camera.
- **Cameras:** top_down and wrist, side by side on one 256 × 512 canvas.
- **Checkpoints:** saved every 2,000 steps in run 2; 10,000, 20,000, 26,000 and 30,000 run on the robot, 26,000 and 30,000 with both raw and EMA weights.
- **Training logs and config:** `flux3_train_ws100.log` (from 0), `flux3_resume.log` (from 10,000); `flux3_lora_vial-sort-ws100_top_down/checkpoints/030000/pretrained_model/train_config.json`.
- **Published:** `sari-abdan/vial-sort-flux3-ws100-30000`: the adapter and its normalisation files, with `ema/` holding the EMA weights, plus `LICENSE.md`, `NOTICE` and `prepare_base.py`. The prepared base (a copy of Black Forest Labs' weights under the FLUX Kommunity License) is not uploaded, on purpose (`scripts/upload_hf.py`).

Settings: `hyper` of its entry (`crispy-falafel`) in `src/data/experiments.json`, read from the `train_config.json` saved with the checkpoint; the Models page shows them. Its learning rate is constant, with no warm-up and no decay, so every checkpoint is a finished model. No augmentation row is recorded for FLUX.

Story:

- **How it was trained:** run 1 from 14 to 15 October (0 to 10,000 steps, 5 h 42 min), run 2 from 18 to 19 October (10,000 to 30,000, 11 h 13 min). Loss fell from 0.249 to 0.087 and was flat from about 8,000 steps (`models.yaml`). The card: one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, about 17 hours in two sessions.
- **Getting it to run, 14 October:** the base repositories were re-published and asked for a new licence click (fixed by pinning the cached revisions); an attention library compiled for CUDA 13 silently fell back to a kernel that tried to allocate 882 GiB (fixed by putting the CUDA 13 runtime on the library path); runs started from an interactive session were killed when the session ended (fixed with a user-level systemd service).
- **Why only two cameras:** FLUX takes a scene view and a wrist view. The overhead camera was chosen on 14 October because it is the only high-angle view, has no people in it, and stays readable in the dark runs.
- **On the robot:** always sync mode, since FLUX has no real-time chunking; it replans every 32 actions with a freeze of about 1.3 s and ran at 15.5 Hz against a 30 Hz target (`src/data/models.json`: 15.46 Hz; the card: in the one session whose rate was logged, the 10k checkpoint on 16 October). At 30,000 steps it grasped the asked vial in 4 of 4 grasps and put it in the asked slot in 1 of 4.
- **Why the destination fails:** source slots are read, destination slots are not; a rack of six slots covers about two of the 8 by 8 cells FLUX divides its picture into (`models.yaml`, `camera_choice`).

### Which camera FLUX looked through

Three FLUX runs, each with a different scene camera: Flux five, Sweet Corn and Crispy Falafel. `models.yaml` states that they also differ in data size, training length and normalisation, so this is a sequence, not a controlled comparison.

The comparison is `camera_choice` of Crispy Falafel's entry in `models.yaml`: an `intro` and one row per run with `run`, `camera` (the scene camera), `data`, `result` and `why`. `scripts/build_data.py` copies it into `src/data/experiments.json`, and the Models page shows it as a table under Crispy Falafel (`src/components/ModelDetail.astro`). Sweet Corn's and Crispy Falafel's own sections give their cameras and outcomes, and Crispy Falafel's ends with why its destination fails. Flux five has no section of its own; its row in [all models](#all-models) gives its cameras, data and steps.
