# The task and the rig

## Scope

This record owns what the robot is asked to do and the hardware it does it with: the SO-101 arm pair, the two racks, their slots and the bin and how they are named, the vials, the five cameras and which models see which, what a command looks like and what a move is, the loop demonstrate → train → run, and the earlier rigs as far as this repository describes them.

It does not own the demonstration data (how many, when, which moves, how the commands are worded, batch and camera differences between recordings): that is the [demonstrations record](../demonstrations/demonstrations.md). It does not own the experiments, the models' training or the scores: those are in the [Lab notes](../../experiments/experiments.md) and on the website's pages. How the website itself is built is the [website record](../website/website.md).

## Where these facts come from

The training, evaluation and robot code is not in this repository. Everything here is read from the website's pages and helpers under `src/`, from the JSON under `src/data/`, from `curation.yaml`, from script headers under `scripts/` and from the dated [Lab notes](../../experiments/experiments.md).

The JSON under `src/data/` is generated by scripts (`scripts/build_data.py`, `scripts/build_ws220.py` and their siblings) from recordings, sheets and logs in the `~/lerobot` checkout on the workstation, which is not in this repository. The numbers below are as recorded in that data at commit 8065309 (2026-11-28) and were not re-derived. Where a number below is arithmetic done for this record, it says so.

## The task

A person types one sentence, the command, and the follower arm takes one vial from the named slot and puts it in the named destination: a slot of either rack or the bin. Nobody steers it while it runs.

| Term (as in `src/lib/glossary.ts`) | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Move | One source slot and one destination, written `R3 → L6` (in the data `R3->L6`): take the vial from slot 3 of the right rack and put it in slot 6 of the left rack. A destination can also be the bin. |
| Source and destination | The source is the slot the vial starts in. The destination is the slot, or the bin, it must end in. |
| Command (prompt) | The sentence typed to the robot. It names the source and the destination by position, never by colour. |
| Wording | One way of phrasing a command. |
| Demonstration | One move recorded by a person driving the leader arm while the follower arm copied it, with all cameras and joint positions logged. |
| Rollout | One autonomous attempt by the robot at one command, from the moment it is started until it stops or the session ends. |
| Session | One launch of the rollout program; it holds several rollouts with the same checkpoint, mode and lighting. |
| Held out | Never recorded on purpose: slots 2 and 5 are never a source, and slots 2, 4 and 5 never a destination. They test whether a model can reach slots no demonstration used. |

The bin is never a source (`src/lib/rackmaps.ts`: "The bin is never a source").

Why the command names a position and not a colour: so that a command stays unambiguous when two vials share a colour; choosing the vial is left to the person typing (`src/pages/datasets.astro`, Command wording tab).

Sources: `src/lib/glossary.ts`, `src/lib/rackmaps.ts`, `src/pages/datasets.astro`, `src/components/Hero.astro`.

## Demonstrate, train, run

Every model on the site came out of the same three steps (`src/pages/index.astro`, chapter 01):

1. **Demonstrate.** A person moves the leader arm and the follower copies it, while five cameras record.
2. **Train.** Three kinds of model learn from the demonstrations: π0.5, FLUX 3 Action and Isaac 0.5.
3. **Run.** The model reads the cameras and the joint angles and drives the six joints. Nobody steers it.

`src/components/Hero.astro` adds that the policy reads the sentence and the camera images, and that nothing else tells it which vial to take or where to put it. `src/pages/method.astro` states that the robot in a rollout is the follower arm of the SO-101 pair on the workstation, with the five cameras.

What was demonstrated is in the [demonstrations record](../demonstrations/demonstrations.md). How rollouts were run and scored, and how each model did, is in the [Lab notes](../../experiments/experiments.md) and on the site's Testing and Results pages (`src/pages/method.astro`, `src/pages/results.astro`).

## Racks, slots and the bin

The table holds two racks of six slots each with the bin between them. Every page draws it the same way: left rack, bin, right rack, slots 1 to 6 from left to right in each (`src/components/RackGlyph.astro`).

| Place | Names | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Left rack | `L1` … `L6` | The rack to the left of the follower arm, read from the arm's point of view. It is the top rack in the overhead camera. |
| Right rack | `R1` … `R6` | The other rack: the bottom one in the overhead camera. |
| Slot number | 1 … 6 | One of the six holes of a rack. Slot 1 is the one nearest the follower arm. |
| Bin | `bin` | Sits between the two racks. Only ever a destination. |

All twelve slot codes and their order are fixed in `src/lib/rackmaps.ts` (`PLACES`: `L1`…`L6`, `R1`…`R6`, `bin`) and in the `slots` lists of `src/data/october.json` and `src/data/ws220.json`. A move is written `<source>-><destination>` in the data (for example `R3->L6`, `L3->bin`) and `R3 → L6` on the pages.

In the 100- and 220-demonstration sets, sources are slots 1, 3, 4 and 6 and destinations are slots 1, 3 and 6 and the bin; slots 2 and 5 are held-out sources and slots 2, 4 and 5 held-out destinations (`heldout_sources`, `heldout_destinations` in `src/data/october.json` and `src/data/ws220.json`; both lists are set by a fixed rule in `scripts/build_data.py` and typed into `scripts/build_ws220.py`, not counted from the data). Coverage move by move is in the [demonstrations record](../demonstrations/demonstrations.md#coverage-move-by-move).

In August the naming was different: the left rack was the one on the left of the side camera (`src/pages/datasets.astro`; see [Earlier rigs](#earlier-rigs)).

Sources: `src/lib/glossary.ts`, `src/pages/datasets.astro` ("In both sets"), `src/components/RackGlyph.astro`, `src/lib/rackmaps.ts`.

## The five cameras

All five record at 30 frames a second (`src/pages/datasets.astro`; `fps` 30 in `src/data/datasets.json` for every set). The key is the name in the recordings (`observation.images.<key>`, `scripts/make_cams.py`); the page name is `CAMERA_NAMES` in `src/lib/site.ts`, listed in this order on every page.

| Key | Page name | Where it sits and what it sees | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `top_down` | Overhead | The only high-angle view. It sees both racks, the bin and the arm at once, with no people in it. | `src/pages/datasets.astro` |
| `left_top` | Across the table | High, across the table. The operator is in view in every frame, on purpose: someone usually stands there during a rollout too. | `src/pages/datasets.astro` |
| `left_bottom` | Near eye level | Near table level; the colour stream of a RealSense camera. The vials look largest here and which holes hold a vial can be read. | `src/pages/datasets.astro` |
| `right` | From the side | A wide view of the workspace from the other side. A person is in view some of the time. The operator sits in the right 16% of this camera in every frame. | `src/pages/datasets.astro`, `curation.yaml` (`hero.crop` comment) |
| `wrist` | On the gripper | Close up, mounted on the gripper. The vial fills the frame during a grasp. | `src/pages/datasets.astro` |
| `rs_depth` | (no page name) | The RealSense depth stream, recorded only in the second sitting of 3 November; no model is trained on it. | `src/data/datasets.json` (`vial-sort-contrast-99-depth`), `src/pages/datasets.astro` |

The photograph of the current rig shows the right, top-down, left-top and left-bottom cameras on stands and the wrist camera on the follower arm (alt text in `src/pages/index.astro`).

Image size: the camera stills on the site are 640 × 480 (`src/pages/datasets.astro`, made by `scripts/make_cams.py` from one demonstration frame); the 9 Nov Lab note compares camera frames at 640×480 and gives the model's input as 224×224. The recording resolution of each camera is not otherwise stated in this repository.

## What a command looks like

A command names the source slot and rack, then the destination slot and rack, or the bin. One recorded command for `R3 → L6`, from `src/data/october.json` (`wordings.example`):

> Move the vial from position 3 of the right rack to slot 6 of the left rack.

The glossary uses the same sentence as its example. The demonstrations use many wordings of each move, drawn from pools of verbs, object names, slot forms, rack forms, bin forms and sentence forms; the pools, their sizes and how a wording was chosen are in the [demonstrations record](../demonstrations/demonstrations.md#how-the-commands-are-worded).

At the robot, the Lab notes write a rollout as `/t <source> <destination>` (for example `/t R3 L1`, `/t L3 bin`) and the operator's observation as `/note <what happened>`, which `tools/assess_rollouts.py` reads back ([Arm test, 4 Nov](../../experiments/2026-11-04-b4000-arm-test/b4000-arm-test.md)). The rollout program itself is not in this repository.
